## IIIF for Researchers in the Natural and Engineering Sciences


The International Image Interoperability Framework (IIIF) has historically been adopted and developed within libraries, archives, and cultural heritage institutions, where it has proven invaluable for the presentation, comparison, and reuse of digitized collections. In recent years, however, IIIF is increasingly being explored as a research infrastructure, for example in the analysis of historical maps and visual materials from engineering and scientific perspectives. This shift raises an important question for the IIIF community: how can IIIF be meaningfully integrated into data-intensive research workflows beyond the heritage domain? In this presentation, we share our experience implementing IIIF within 4TU.ResearchData, the trusted research data repository for the Natural and Engineering Sciences in the Netherlands. At 4TU.ReserachData, we have deployed a IIIF server that supports both the Image API and the Presentation API, fully integrated into a custom Python-based backend tailored to research data management rather than collection curation. Our implementation treats IIIF not merely as a visualization layer, but as an interoperability framework embedded within the lifecycle of research data. We present concrete use cases from ongoing research at Delft University of Technology, focusing on experimental imaging datasets and historical maps. In these cases, IIIF manifests function as machine-readable research objects that organize images, metadata, and structural relationships in a way that supports downstream reuse. We envision that researchers could compare datasets across experiments, aggregate images from multiple sources, and enrich data through annotations that are relevant for scientific interpretation . This work offers a new perspective on IIIF as a bridge between research data repositories and active research workflows. By embedding IIIF into a repository serving the Natural and Engineering Sciences, we demonstrate how IIIF can support FAIR principles, particularly interoperability and reusability, in domains that traditionally rely on bespoke formats and discipline-specific tooling. For the IIIF community, this contribution broadens the conversation around IIIF adoption by presenting a concrete, production-level implementation in a scientific research context. It invites discussion on how IIIF specifications, tools, and community practices can evolve to better support research-driven use cases, and how lessons from the heritage sector can inform interoperable infrastructures for data-intensive science.
