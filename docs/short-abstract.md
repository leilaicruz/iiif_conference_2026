The International Image Interoperability Framework (IIIF) has traditionally been used in libraries, archives, and cultural heritage institutions to support the presentation and reuse of digitized collections. More recently, IIIF is attracting interest as a possible infrastructure for research workflows, for example in the study of historical maps and scientific imagery. This raises the question of how IIIF can be integrated into research data repositories serving data-intensive scientific domains. In this presentation, we describe the implementation of IIIF within 4TU.ResearchData, the trusted research data repository for the Natural and Engineering Sciences in the Netherlands. A IIIF server supporting the Image and Presentation APIs has been integrated into a custom Python-based backend focused on research data management rather than collection curation. IIIF is approached primarily as an interoperability layer that can expose and structure image-based research data. We discuss early, exploratory scenarios from Delft University of Technology to illustrate how IIIF manifests could support future activities such as comparison, aggregation, and annotation. This work highlights IIIF’s potential role in supporting FAIR principles, particularly interoperability and reuse, and invites discussion within the IIIF community on research-oriented use cases beyond the heritage domain.
